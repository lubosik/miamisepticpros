Understanding the differences between these scales helps determine the most appropriate approach for different situations.</p><h2 id="what-it-is-and-why-it-matters" class="text-h2 font-serif-headings font-semibold text-charcoal mt-12 mb-4">What it is and why it matters</h2><p class="text-body text-body-text mb-6 leading-relaxed">On-site scale systems serve individual properties, with each property having its own treatment and disposal system. Neighborhood-scale systems (also called cluster systems) serve multiple properties, collecting wastewater from several homes or buildings and treating it at a central location before disposal. Both approaches are decentralized (not connected to municipal sewers) but operate at different scales.</p><p class="text-body text-body-text mb-6 leading-relaxed">The <a href="https://www.epa.gov/septic" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="text-accent-green hover:text-accent-green-hover underline">EPA Decentralized Wastewater Systems Manual</a> explains that scale selection depends on factors like property density, site conditions, cost considerations, and management requirements. The <a href="https://www.miamidade.gov/permits/septic-systems.asp" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="text-accent-green hover:text-accent-green-hover underline">Miami-Dade County DERM</a> evaluates scale options when approving decentralized system designs.</p>

<h2 id="technical-breakdown" class="text-h2 font-serif-headings font-semibold text-charcoal mt-12 mb-4">Technical breakdown</h2><p class="text-body text-body-text mb-6 leading-relaxed"><strong>On-site scale systems:</strong> On-site systems serve individual properties, with each property having its own septic tank or treatment unit and drainfield. These systems are self-contained and require individual maintenance. The <a href="https://www.epa.gov/septic" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="text-accent-green hover:text-accent-green-hover underline">EPA</a> explains that on-site systems are appropriate for properties with adequate space, suitable soil conditions, and sufficient separation from water sources.</p><p class="text-body text-body-text mb-6 leading-relaxed"><strong>Neighborhood-scale systems:</strong> Neighborhood-scale (cluster) systems collect wastewater from multiple properties through a collection network and treat it at a central treatment facility before disposal. These systems can serve small neighborhoods, subdivisions, or groups of properties. The <a href="https://edis.ifas.ufl.edu/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="text-accent-green hover:text-accent-green-hover underline">University of Florida IFAS Extension</a> explains that cluster systems are appropriate when individual on-site systems are not feasible due to site constraints.</p><p class="text-body text-body-text mb-6 leading-relaxed"><strong>Scale selection factors:</strong> Factors that influence scale selection include property density (higher density may favor cluster systems), site conditions (restrictive soils may require cluster systems), cost considerations (cluster systems may be more cost-effective for multiple properties), management requirements (cluster systems require centralized management), and regulatory requirements (local regulations may favor one scale over another).</p><p class="text-body text-body-text mb-6 leading-relaxed"><strong>Cost considerations:</strong> On-site systems typically have lower initial costs per property but require individual maintenance. Neighborhood-scale systems may have higher initial costs but can be more cost-effective for multiple properties when shared infrastructure reduces per-property costs. The <a href="https://www.epa.gov/septic" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="text-accent-green hover:text-accent-green-hover underline">EPA</a> explains that cost analysis should consider both initial and long-term operational costs.</p><p class="text-body text-body-text mb-6 leading-relaxed"><strong>Management requirements:</strong> On-site systems require individual property owners to maintain their systems. Neighborhood-scale systems require centralized management, which may be provided by a homeowners association, management company, or utility. The <a href="https://www.floridahealth.gov/environmental-health/onsite-sewage/index.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="text-accent-green hover:text-accent-green-hover underline">Florida DOH</a> requires management plans for cluster systems to ensure proper operation and maintenance.</p><p class="text-body text-body-text mb-6 leading-relaxed"><strong>Site condition factors:</strong> Site conditions that may favor neighborhood-scale systems include restrictive soils, high water tables, limited lot sizes, and proximity to sensitive water sources. The <a href="https://www.miamidade.gov/permits/septic-systems.asp" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="text-accent-green hover:text-accent-green-hover underline">Miami-Dade County DERM</a> evaluates site conditions when determining appropriate system scale.</p><h2 id="south-florida-notes-local-relevance" class="text-h2 font-serif-headings font-semibold text-charcoal mt-12 mb-4">South Florida notes (local relevance)</h2><p class="text-body text-body-text mb-6 leading-relaxed">Miami-Dade County's high property density, high water table, and proximity to sensitive water sources (Biscayne Aquifer) create situations where neighborhood-scale systems may be appropriate. The <a href="https://www.miamidade.gov/permits/septic-systems.asp" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="text-accent-green hover:text-accent-green-hover underline">Miami-Dade County DERM</a> evaluates scale options based on local conditions and may require cluster systems in areas where individual on-site systems are not feasible.</p><p class="text-body text-body-text mb-6 leading-relaxed">The county's coastal location and high water table mean that some properties may not have adequate space or soil conditions for individual <a href="/services/drain-field-installation" class="text-accent-green hover:text-accent-green-hover underline">drainfield installation</a>. In these cases, neighborhood-scale systems with centralized treatment and disposal may be required. Professional <a href="/services/septic-system-design" class="text-accent-green hover:text-accent-green-hover underline">septic system design</a> services evaluate site conditions to determine appropriate system scale.</p><p class="text-body text-body-text mb-6 leading-relaxed">Miami-Dade DERM requires proper management plans for neighborhood-scale systems to ensure effective operation and protect groundwater quality. Both on-site and neighborhood-scale systems must comply with DERM regulations, but management requirements differ based on system scale.</p>

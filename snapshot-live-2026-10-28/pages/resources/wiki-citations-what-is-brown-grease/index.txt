Unlike yellow grease (used cooking oil), brown grease contains food particles, water, and other contaminants that make it more difficult to process and recycle.</p><h2 id="what-it-is-and-why-it-matters" class="text-h2 font-serif-headings font-semibold text-charcoal mt-12 mb-4">What it is and why it matters</h2><p class="text-body text-body-text mb-6 leading-relaxed">Brown grease forms when FOG (fats, oils, and grease) from commercial kitchens enters grease traps along with food solids and wastewater. As these materials separate in the trap, brown grease accumulates as a semi-solid mixture containing FOG, food particles, water, and other organic matter. This composition distinguishes brown grease from yellow grease, which is relatively pure used cooking oil.</p><p class="text-body text-body-text mb-6 leading-relaxed">The <a href="https://www.epa.gov/npdes/pretreatment-program-requirements" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="text-accent-green hover:text-accent-green-hover underline">EPA Pretreatment Program</a> recognizes brown grease as a type of FOG that requires proper management to prevent sewer blockages. <h2 id="technical-breakdown" class="text-h2 font-serif-headings font-semibold text-charcoal mt-12 mb-4">Technical breakdown</h2><p class="text-body text-body-text mb-6 leading-relaxed"><strong>Composition:</strong> Brown grease consists of approximately 15-30% FOG, 5-15% food solids, and 50-80% water, along with other organic matter. This mixed composition results from the combination of cooking oils, animal fats, food scraps, and wastewater that enters grease traps from commercial kitchens.</p><p class="text-body text-body-text mb-6 leading-relaxed"><strong>Formation process:</strong> When wastewater containing FOG, food solids, and other organic matter enters a grease trap, gravity separation occurs. FOG floats to the top, food solids settle to the bottom, and water remains in the middle. Brown grease forms as the FOG layer mixes with food particles and water, creating a semi-solid, brown-colored material.</p><p class="text-body text-body-text mb-6 leading-relaxed"><strong>Characteristics:</strong> Brown grease has a high water content, contains food solids and organic matter, and has a lower energy value than yellow grease due to its mixed composition. The <a href="https://www.floridahealth.gov/environmental-health/onsite-sewage/index.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="text-accent-green hover:text-accent-green-hover underline">Florida Department of Health</a> explains that brown grease's composition makes it unsuitable for direct use as biodiesel feedstock without extensive processing.</p><p class="text-body text-body-text mb-6 leading-relaxed"><strong>Difference from yellow grease:</strong> Yellow grease is relatively pure used cooking oil collected directly from fryers and cooking equipment, containing minimal food solids and water. Brown grease, in contrast, is the FOG that accumulates in grease traps after mixing with food solids and wastewater, resulting in a more contaminated material that requires different handling and disposal methods.</p><p class="text-body text-body-text mb-6 leading-relaxed"><strong>Disposal requirements:</strong> Brown grease must be disposed of by licensed waste haulers who transport it to approved treatment facilities. The <a href="https://www.epa.gov/npdes/pretreatment-program-requirements" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="text-accent-green hover:text-accent-green-hover underline">EPA</a> and local regulations prohibit dumping brown grease into sewers, drains, or the environment due to its potential to cause blockages and contamination.</p><h2 id="south-florida-notes-local-relevance" class="text-h2 font-serif-headings font-semibold text-charcoal mt-12 mb-4">South Florida notes (local relevance)</h2><p class="text-body text-body-text mb-6 leading-relaxed">Miami-Dade County restaurants must properly manage brown grease through regular <a href="/services/grease-interceptor-cleaning" class="text-accent-green hover:text-accent-green-hover underline">grease trap cleaning</a> and proper disposal. The <a href="https://www.miamidade.gov/permits/grease-traps.asp" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="text-accent-green hover:text-accent-green-hover underline">Miami-Dade County DERM</a> requires that brown grease be removed by licensed waste haulers and disposed of at approved facilities to prevent sewer blockages and environmental contamination.</p><p class="text-body text-body-text mb-6 leading-relaxed">The county's high concentration of restaurants means significant volumes of brown grease are generated daily. Proper <a href="/services/grease-trap-installation-for-restaurants" class="text-accent-green hover:text-accent-green-hover underline">grease trap installation</a> and maintenance ensure brown grease is effectively captured and removed before it can enter municipal sewer systems, protecting infrastructure and reducing costs.</p><p class="text-body text-body-text mb-6 leading-relaxed">Miami-Dade restaurants must comply with DERM requirements for brown grease management, including regular trap cleaning, proper disposal documentation, and compliance with disposal regulations.

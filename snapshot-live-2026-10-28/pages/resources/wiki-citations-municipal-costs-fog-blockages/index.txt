<h2 id="technical-breakdown" class="text-h2 font-serif-headings font-semibold text-charcoal mt-12 mb-4">Technical breakdown</h2><p class="text-body text-body-text mb-6 leading-relaxed"><strong>Emergency maintenance costs:</strong> When FOG blockages occur, municipalities must respond quickly to prevent sewage backups and overflows. Emergency response includes dispatching crews, using specialized equipment like jet trucks and vacuum trucks, and working around the clock to clear blockages. The <a href="https://www.nacwa.org/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="text-accent-green hover:text-accent-green-hover underline">National Association of Clean Water Agencies</a> reports that emergency FOG blockage response can cost thousands of dollars per incident, with costs increasing significantly when blockages cause overflows.</p><p class="text-body text-body-text mb-6 leading-relaxed"><strong>Sewer line repair costs:</strong> FOG blockages can damage sewer infrastructure, requiring repairs to pipes, manholes, and pump stations. Blockages increase pressure in sewer lines, potentially causing pipe failures, joint separations, and structural damage. Municipalities must repair or replace damaged infrastructure, with costs ranging from thousands to hundreds of thousands of dollars depending on the extent of damage.</p><p class="text-body text-body-text mb-6 leading-relaxed"><strong>Environmental cleanup costs:</strong> When FOG blockages cause sewage overflows, municipalities face environmental cleanup costs including street cleaning, waterway remediation, and potential regulatory fines. The <a href="https://www.epa.gov/npdes/pretreatment-program-requirements" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="text-accent-green hover:text-accent-green-hover underline">EPA</a> can impose significant penalties for unauthorized discharges, and municipalities must remediate environmental damage caused by overflows.</p><p class="text-body text-body-text mb-6 leading-relaxed"><strong>Preventive maintenance costs:</strong> Municipalities invest in preventive maintenance programs to reduce FOG blockages, including sewer line cleaning, inspection programs, and public education campaigns. While these programs have costs, they are far less expensive than emergency response and repairs when blockages occur.</p><p class="text-body text-body-text mb-6 leading-relaxed"><strong>Compliance and enforcement costs:</strong> Municipalities must enforce grease trap requirements, including inspections, permitting, and compliance monitoring. These programs require staff time and resources but are essential to prevent FOG from entering sewer systems and creating costly blockages.</p><h2 id="south-florida-notes-local-relevance" class="text-h2 font-serif-headings font-semibold text-charcoal mt-12 mb-4">South Florida notes (local relevance)</h2><p class="text-body text-body-text mb-6 leading-relaxed">Miami-Dade County faces unique challenges with FOG management due to the high concentration of restaurants and tourism. The <a href="https://www.miamidade.gov/water/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="text-accent-green hover:text-accent-green-hover underline">Miami-Dade County Water and Sewer Department</a> invests in FOG prevention programs to protect sewer infrastructure and reduce costs associated with blockages and overflows.</p><p class="text-body text-body-text mb-6 leading-relaxed">The county's coastal location means FOG-related sewage overflows can impact sensitive marine environments, increasing environmental cleanup costs. Proper <a href="/services/grease-trap-installation-for-restaurants" class="text-accent-green hover:text-accent-green-hover underline">grease trap installation</a> and regular <a href="/services/grease-interceptor-cleaning" class="text-accent-green hover:text-accent-green-hover underline">grease trap cleaning</a> by commercial facilities are essential to prevent FOG from entering the municipal system and creating costly blockages.</p><p class="text-body text-body-text mb-6 leading-relaxed">Miami-Dade DERM enforces strict grease trap requirements to protect municipal infrastructure and reduce costs.

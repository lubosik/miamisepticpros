<h2 id="technical-breakdown" class="text-h2 font-serif-headings font-semibold text-charcoal mt-12 mb-4">Technical breakdown</h2><p class="text-body text-body-text mb-6 leading-relaxed"><strong>Drainfield disposal:</strong> Drainfields (also called leach fields or absorption fields) are the most common effluent disposal method for decentralized systems. Effluent flows from the septic tank or treatment unit into perforated pipes buried in trenches or beds filled with gravel. The effluent percolates through the soil, where it receives additional treatment through filtration and biological processes. The <a href="https://www.epa.gov/septic" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="text-accent-green hover:text-accent-green-hover underline">EPA</a> explains that drainfields require suitable soil conditions, adequate depth to water table, and proper sizing based on percolation rates.</p><p class="text-body text-body-text mb-6 leading-relaxed"><strong>Spray irrigation:</strong> Spray irrigation systems distribute treated effluent over land surfaces using sprinklers or spray heads. This method is suitable for areas with adequate land area, appropriate soil conditions, and sufficient separation from water sources. The <a href="https://edis.ifas.ufl.edu/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="text-accent-green hover:text-accent-green-hover underline">University of Florida IFAS Extension</a> explains that spray irrigation requires proper system design, regular maintenance, and compliance with regulatory requirements for application rates and setbacks.</p><p class="text-body text-body-text mb-6 leading-relaxed"><strong>Evapotranspiration systems:</strong> Evapotranspiration (ET) systems use shallow, lined beds filled with sand or other media. Effluent is applied to the bed surface, where it evaporates or is taken up by plants. ET systems are suitable for areas with low rainfall, high evaporation rates, and restrictive soil conditions. The <a href="https://www.epa.gov/septic" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="text-accent-green hover:text-accent-green-hover underline">EPA</a> notes that ET systems require careful design and are typically used in arid or semi-arid climates.</p><p class="text-body text-body-text mb-6 leading-relaxed"><strong>Surface discharge:</strong> Surface discharge involves releasing treated effluent directly to surface waters, such as streams or ponds. This method requires advanced treatment to meet water quality standards and is subject to strict regulatory requirements. The <a href="https://www.floridahealth.gov/environmental-health/onsite-sewage/index.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="text-accent-green hover:text-accent-green-hover underline">Florida DOH</a> regulates surface discharge and requires permits and monitoring to ensure water quality protection.</p><p class="text-body text-body-text mb-6 leading-relaxed"><strong>Site condition factors:</strong> Site conditions that determine appropriate disposal methods include soil type and percolation rate, depth to seasonal high water table, available land area, slope and topography, proximity to water sources, and local climate conditions. The <a href="https://www.miamidade.gov/permits/septic-systems.asp" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="text-accent-green hover:text-accent-green-hover underline">Miami-Dade County DERM</a> evaluates these factors when approving effluent disposal methods for decentralized systems.</p><p class="text-body text-body-text mb-6 leading-relaxed"><strong>Regulatory requirements:</strong> Different disposal methods have different regulatory requirements for design, installation, operation, and monitoring. The <a href="https://www.epa.gov/septic" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="text-accent-green hover:text-accent-green-hover underline">EPA</a> and state/local health departments establish requirements to ensure effluent disposal protects public health and the environment.</p><h2 id="south-florida-notes-local-relevance" class="text-h2 font-serif-headings font-semibold text-charcoal mt-12 mb-4">South Florida notes (local relevance)</h2><p class="text-body text-body-text mb-6 leading-relaxed">Miami-Dade County's high water table, sandy soils, and coastal location create unique challenges for effluent disposal. The <a href="https://www.miamidade.gov/permits/septic-systems.asp" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="text-accent-green hover:text-accent-green-hover underline">Miami-Dade County DERM</a> requires careful evaluation of site conditions to determine appropriate disposal methods, with drainfields being the most common method for conventional systems.</p><p class="text-body text-body-text mb-6 leading-relaxed">The county's high water table often requires <a href="/services/drain-field-installation" class="text-accent-green hover:text-accent-green-hover underline">drainfield installation</a> modifications or alternative disposal methods. In some cases, <a href="/services/advanced-treatment-unit-installation" class="text-accent-green hover:text-accent-green-hover underline">advanced treatment unit installation</a> may be required to produce effluent suitable for disposal in challenging site conditions.</p><p class="text-body text-body-text mb-6 leading-relaxed">Miami-Dade DERM enforces strict requirements for effluent disposal to protect the Biscayne Aquifer, the region's primary drinking water source. Professional <a href="/services/septic-system-design" class="text-accent-green hover:text-accent-green-hover underline">septic system design</a> services ensure effluent disposal methods comply with DERM requirements and protect groundwater quality.</p>

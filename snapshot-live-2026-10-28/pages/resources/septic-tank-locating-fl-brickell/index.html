What Is Septic Tank Locating?

Septic tank locating is a professional service that finds buried septic tanks using specialized equipment and techniques. When tanks are buried underground and their exact location is unknown, professional locators use electronic locators, ground-penetrating radar (GPR), manual probing, and visual inspection to determine precise tank location, boundaries, and depth.

The locating process typically includes using electronic locators that trace signals from sewer lines to find tanks, ground-penetrating radar that sends radio waves to detect underground structures, manual probing with specialized rods to physically locate tank edges, and visual inspection for ground variations or vegetation patterns. Professional locators combine multiple methods for accuracy, choosing techniques based on property conditions and soil type.

After locating the tank, professionals mark tank boundaries and lid locations, measure depth to the top of the tank, and provide a detailed report with location information. This documentation is valuable for property records, future maintenance, and preventing accidental damage during construction or landscaping projects.

How Septic Tank Locating Works

The locating process begins with a property assessment to determine the best locating methods. Licensed professionals evaluate property conditions, soil type, available access points, and any existing documentation. This assessment helps determine which locating methods will be most effective.

Next, professionals use specialized equipment to locate the tank. Electronic locators trace signals from sewer lines or cleanouts to find tanks. Ground-penetrating radar sends radio waves into the ground to detect underground structures and provide images showing tank location, size, and depth. Manual probing uses specialized rods to physically locate tank edges in soft soils.

After locating the tank, professionals mark the exact location, measure depth, and document findings. They provide a detailed report with location information, depth measurements, and recommendations for access improvements if needed. This documentation is valuable for property records and future maintenance.

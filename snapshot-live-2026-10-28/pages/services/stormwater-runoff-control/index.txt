<h2 id="what-is">What is stormwater runoff?</h2>

<p>Stormwater runoff is rain and tidal water flowing over driveways, lawns, sidewalks, and streets collecting debris, chemicals, fertilizers, auto fluids, and other pollutants before entering stormwater systems. Stormwater runoff represents major source of pollution in waterways threatening environmental health and local ecosystems.</p>

<strong>Pollution sources:</strong>
<ul>
<li>Debris and litter from streets</li>
<li>Chemicals from lawns and gardens</li>
<li>Fertilizers and pesticides</li>
<li>Auto fluids from vehicles</li>
<li>Sediment and suspended soils</li>
<li>Heavy metals from various sources</li>
</ul>

<strong>Runoff characteristics:</strong>
<ul>
<li>High concentrations of sediments</li>
<li>Suspended soils and nutrients</li>
<li>Phosphorus and nitrogen compounds</li>
<li>Heavy metals contamination</li>
<li>Oil and grease from vehicles</li>
</ul>

<strong>Critical pollution window:</strong>
<ul>
<li>First inch of runoff carries 90% of pollution</li>
<li>Treatment of first flush essential</li>
<li>Initial runoff most contaminated</li>
<li>Focusing treatment on first inch critical</li>
</ul>

<p>Understanding stormwater runoff characteristics essential for effective control <a href="https://www.epa.gov/septic" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">(EPA)</a>. First flush treatment addresses majority of pollution.</p>

<h2 id="faqs">Frequently Asked Questions</h2>

<h3>How much does stormwater runoff control cost?</h3>

<p>Stormwater runoff control in Miami typically costs $3,000-$20,000 for property-level systems depending on size, BMP types, pollution control devices, and compliance requirements. Basic systems cost $3,000-$8,000. Standard systems cost $8,000-$15,000. Comprehensive systems cost $15,000-$30,000. Costs include BMP installation, pollution control devices, first flush treatment, design, and permits.</p>

<h3>What is stormwater runoff control?</h3>

<p>Stormwater runoff control manages rain and tidal water flowing over surfaces collecting pollutants before entering stormwater systems. Control serves three primary objectives: draining stormwater quickly preventing flooding, cleaning contaminants before discharge into receiving waters, and managing stormwater to recharge groundwater supplies. The first inch of runoff carries 90% of pollution making treatment of first flush critical.</p>

<h3>Why is stormwater runoff control important?</h3>

<p>Stormwater runoff control important for preventing contamination of waterways, protecting Biscayne Bay water quality, reducing pollutants entering ecosystems, draining stormwater quickly preventing flooding, cleaning contaminants before discharge, managing stormwater for groundwater recharge, and meeting NPDES permit requirements and Miami-Dade County ordinances. Control addresses pollution, flooding, and compliance concerns.</p>

<h3>What are stormwater best management practices?</h3>

<p>Best management practices include pollution reduction through public education and good housekeeping practices, surface drainage and flood protection, erosion and sedimentation control, enhanced aesthetics and recreational opportunities, reuse of stormwater as valuable water resource, treatment BMPs like permeable surfaces and vegetated areas, and treatment of first inch of runoff addressing 90% of pollution.</p>

<h3>How do you control stormwater runoff?</h3>

<p>Control stormwater runoff through structural BMPs like permeable pavements and detention ponds, vegetated BMPs like rain gardens and bioswales, pollution control devices like oil separators and sediment filters, source control through public education and good housekeeping, and treatment of first flush runoff. Professional evaluation determines best approach for property needs.</p>

<h3>What maintenance do stormwater control systems need?</h3>

<p>Maintenance requirements include regular cleaning of BMPs and treatment areas, removing accumulated debris preventing blockages, inspection of system components and pollution control devices, cleaning pollution control devices maintaining effectiveness, and vegetation management maintaining vegetated BMPs. Regular maintenance ensures continued effectiveness and system reliability.</p>

<h3>What are NPDES permit requirements?</h3>

<p>Miami-Dade County operates under NPDES permit covering county and over 30 local municipalities, establishing water quality standards and regulating discharge from municipal stormwater systems. NPDES requires operators to develop best management practices improving quality of stormwater discharges. Compliance with NPDES requirements essential for legal operation and avoiding violations and fines.</p>

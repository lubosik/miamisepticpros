<h2 id="when-emergency">When should I call for emergency service?</h2>

<p>Understanding when something is a true emergency helps you get the right response and know when immediate service is justified.</p>

<strong>True emergencies (call immediately):</strong>
<ul>
<li>Sewage backing up into your home through drains or toilets</li>
<li>Wastewater in living areas, creating health hazards</li>
<li>Strong sewage odors inside your home</li>
<li>Sewage surfacing in your yard or around drainfield</li>

<li>System problems right after a major storm</li>
<li>Real estate inspection deadlines approaching</li>
</ul>

<strong>Routine situations (schedule during normal business hours):</strong>
<ul>
<li>Regular maintenance pumping</li>
<li>Preventive cleaning</li>
<li>Routine inspections</li>
<li>Non-urgent repairs</li>
<li>General questions or consultations</li>
</ul>

<p>If you're not sure whether your situation is an emergency, call us. We'd rather come out and find it's not urgent than have you wait when immediate service is needed. Health hazards and sewage backups always need immediate attention.</p>

<h2>Emergency services after hurricanes and storms</h2>

<p>Miami's hurricane season brings unique septic emergencies. Heavy rain, flooding, and storm surge can overwhelm septic systems, requiring immediate emergency service.</p>

<strong>Common post-storm septic problems:</strong>

<p><strong>Flooded tanks and drainfields:</strong> Heavy rain and flooding can saturate drainfields and fill tanks with floodwater. Systems can't function when completely flooded, causing immediate backups and failures.</p>

<p><strong>Debris in systems:</strong> Storms wash sand, dirt, leaves, and debris into septic systems. This can clog pipes, fill tanks, and damage components. Emergency pumping and cleaning remove this debris.</p>

<p><strong>Structural damage:</strong> Flooding, falling trees, or shifting ground can damage tank structures, pipes, or drainfields. Emergency crews assess damage and make temporary repairs to restore basic function.</p>

<p><strong>Power outages:</strong> Systems with pumps or advanced treatment units can fail during power outages. Emergency crews can provide backup solutions or temporary fixes until power is restored.</p>

<strong>What emergency crews do after storms:</strong>
<ul>
<li>Immediate damage assessment</li>
<li>Emergency pumping of flooded systems</li>
<li>Debris removal from tanks and drainfields</li>
<li>Assessment of system functionality</li>
<li>Temporary repairs to restore basic service</li>
<li>Recommendations for full restoration</li>
</ul>

<strong>When to call after a storm:</strong>
<ul>
<li>If sewage is backing up into your home</li>
<li>If drainfield area is completely flooded and not draining</li>
<li>If you notice strong sewage odors</li>
<li>If system completely stops working</li>
<li>If you see visible damage to tank or pipes</li>
</ul>

<p>After major storms, emergency crews are very busy. They prioritize health hazards and properties with sewage in living areas. If your situation isn't an immediate health hazard, you may wait longer, but crews work continuously to address all emergencies.</p>

<h2>Health hazards requiring immediate emergency service</h2>

<p>Some septic problems create immediate health risks requiring emergency service to protect your family and neighbors.</p>

<strong>Sewage in living areas:</strong>
<p>If wastewater backs up into your home through drains or toilets, this creates immediate health hazards. Raw sewage contains harmful bacteria and pathogens. Emergency crews respond immediately to stop backups, remove contamination, and make your home safe again, following <a href="https://www.floridahealth.gov/environmental-health/onsite-sewage/" class="text-blue-600 hover:underline" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Florida Department of Health safety protocols</a>.</p>

<strong>Surfacing waste:</strong>
<p>If sewage surfaces in your yard or around your drainfield, it exposes waste to air and can contaminate soil and nearby water sources, as noted in <a href="https://floridadep.gov/water/domestic-wastewater/" class="text-blue-600 hover:underline" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Florida DEP environmental regulations</a>. This requires immediate pumping and containment to prevent spread and protect public health.</p>

<strong>Contaminated water sources:</strong>
<p>If you have a well and suspect septic contamination, or if neighbors report contamination issues, immediate assessment and service is needed to protect water supplies.</p>

<strong>Strong odors in living areas:</strong>
<p>Persistent sewage odors inside your home often mean waste is backing up somewhere in your system. This indicates system failure that needs immediate attention before conditions worsen.</p>

<strong>What emergency crews do for health hazards:</strong>
<ul>
<li>Immediate response with highest priority</li>
<li>Stop the source of contamination</li>
<li>Contain and clean up waste</li>
<li>Assess public health risks</li>
<li>Make recommendations for permanent solutions</li>
<li>Document situation for health department if needed</li>
</ul>

<p>Health hazards are always highest priority for emergency crews. If you have sewage in your home or surfacing waste, call immediately and explain that it's a health hazard. Crews will respond as quickly as possible.</p>
